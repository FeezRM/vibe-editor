# TEARDOWN — what actually performs

Frame-by-frame analysis of three viral reels (`samples/`), 45s / 47s / 51s, all 9:16.
Transcribed locally and sampled at 1fps, with full-resolution frames pulled at every
graphic beat. **Colours below are measured off the pixels, not guessed.**

This file exists because the previous house style failed. Read §0 before anything else.

---

## 0. The failure this replaces

The old system said *"graphics are instrumentation, not decoration"* and produced dark
plates with engraved text on them — a claim in quotes with a line through it, a header
reading `THE ADVICE`, five numbered empty slots. Every one of those graphics was **the
caption again, in a different font.** It restated the audio instead of depicting it.

None of the three reference reels contains a single abstract plate. Not one.

> ### The rule that replaces it
>
> **SHOW THE NOUN.**
>
> If the script names a thing — a profile, a hook, a story, a reel, a DM, a document, a
> view count — put **that thing** on screen, recreated faithfully enough to be recognised
> in under half a second. If the script names a quantity, a rank, or a change, draw the
> **actual chart or counter**, with real axes and real units.
>
> A graphic that could be replaced by its own caption is not a graphic. Delete it.

Test every proposed graphic against one question: *if I muted the video and removed the
captions, would this frame still communicate the point?* If the answer is no, it is
decoration.

## 1. What the three reels are

| | Subject | Core device |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | "your profile is in the top 80% / 50% / 30% / 20% / 10% / 1%" | a **rebuilt Instagram profile** that fills in element by element + a **tier pyramid** that gains a layer per percentile |
| **S2** | "you went viral — cash it in within 72 hours" | **Instagram story/feed UI** mockups, a **72:00:00 countdown**, engagement counters ticking up |
| **S3** | "stop using clichéd hooks — change a couple of words" | a **phone in a hand** playing bad hooks, a **wall of identical reels**, two real reels **side by side with view counts** |

All three are the same content genre as this repo's user. All three solve it the same way.

## 2. The graphic vocabulary (what they actually build)

Ranked by how often it appears. This replaces the old 20-block catalogue.

### 2.1 App UI recreations — the workhorse, ~60% of all graphic time

They rebuild the **actual interface** being discussed, in Instagram's real dark mode:

- **Profile** (S1): avatar ring, name row, bio lines, link, highlight circles, post grid.
  Starts as grey skeleton bars and **fills in progressively** — the avatar becomes a real
  headshot, the name types in, each bio line lands one at a time with its emoji bullet,
  the highlight circles fill with icons and labels, the grid populates with thumbnails.
  **The state persists across appearances** — it never resets. Six separate cutaways add
  up to one continuous build.
- **Story** (S2): full-bleed story frame with avatar, handle, ✕, and a **text box being
  typed into** — `"Hey [Name], What's It Like Working With You?"` appears character by
  character with `[Name]` in blue.
- **Feed / stories tray** (S2): the home feed with the stories rail, plus a red engagement
  pill whose numbers **count up** (3.4K→5.7K comments, 4.2K→7.0K likes, 2.1K→3.5K shares).
- **Reel card** (S2, S3): a rounded thumbnail with a **view-count badge** (`👁 1.2M`).
  This one tiny component does enormous work — see §2.2.

**Why it works:** the viewer already knows this interface. Recognition is instant and
costs zero explanation. A rebuilt UI is legible in a way an invented diagram never is.

### 2.2 Real content + a metric badge

The single highest-leverage device in all three reels.

They show **actual screenshots or recordings of real videos** with a view count attached:

- S3 puts the losing hook (`Here are the 3 SECRETS to FAT LOSS`, **7K**) beside the winning
  one (`How To MELT Fat Off Your Body`, **1.2M**). Same claim, two numbers. The argument is
  made entirely by the pair.
- S1's post grid carries `28.8M`, `8.6M`, `7.8M` on the thumbnails.
- S2 highlights the viral reel in a row of three by outlining it gold and showing `1.2M`
  against `10.7K` and `11.4K`.

**The comparison is the content.** Two artefacts, two numbers, winner marked. Nothing is
asserted — it is shown and the viewer does the arithmetic.

### 2.3 Device frame in a real hand

S3 composites a **phone into actual footage of a hand holding it**, screen content playing
inside. It reads as physical rather than as a graphic overlay. The phone content is a full
IG reel UI — like/comment/share rail, profile row, Follow button, bottom nav.

### 2.4 The content wall

S3 shows *"everyone's heard these a million times"* as a **grid of a dozen near-identical
reels**, all with the same clichéd hook, drifting. Volume and sameness made literal.

### 2.5 Charts that are actually charts

- **Retention curve** (S3, over the hook): a red line on a dark card, y-axis labelled
  `100% / 50% / 0%`, x-axis `00:00 → 01:10`, falling off a cliff in the first seconds and
  flat-lining. **Real axes, real units.**
- **Tier pyramid** (S1): a stacked pyramid gaining one layer per percentile named, colour
  ramping bottom-to-top `red → orange → yellow → light green → green → violet`, a star at
  the apex, and the whole stack glowing at the finish.
- **Countdown** (S2): `72:00:00 / HOURS MINUTES SECONDS` ticking to `71:59:59`.

### 2.6 The semantic colour wash

S3's best trick, and nearly free: when he says a **bad** phrase the entire background
behind him floods **red**; when he says the **good** version it floods **green**. Full-frame
radial glow, heavy vignette, subject rim-lit. The caption keyword takes the same colour.

`super easy` → red. `disgustingly easy` → green. `super simple` → red. `mind-numbingly
simple` → green.

The viewer knows which is endorsed before reading a word.

### 2.7 Annotation on the artefact

S3 draws a green box around the word `MELT` **on the mockup itself** and writes
`unique within it's context` beside it. The annotation points at a real thing on screen.

### 2.8 The CTA, shown not written

S1 ends on a real profile card with a **Follow button that visibly gets pressed** and turns
to `Following`. S3 ends on a **Notion-style document** floating in perspective, showing the
actual lead magnet with real rows.

## 3. Placement — graphics are BIG

The old system docked everything into a 320px strip at the bottom of frame. That is not
what these do.

| Mode | Share | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-frame takeover** | ~40% | Cut away from the face entirely. The graphic *is* the shot — or the footage is embedded *inside* the graphic (S2 puts the talking head in an IG feed post). |
| **Beside the head** | ~30% | One object at head height, in whichever half of frame the subject is not occupying. He points at it. |
| **Background replacement** | ~15% | Colour wash, reel-grid wallpaper — *behind* the subject, not over him. |
| **Chest-height inline object** | ~10% | A DM bar, a `👁 496` counter. Small, centred, sitting in the caption slot. |
| **Full-width bottom band** | ~5% | **Rare.** One instance across all three reels. |

**Measured from S3's hook frame (720×1280):** the retention card spans x 15%→85%,
y 57%→75%. Converted to 1080×1920 that is a card ~756px wide and ~345px tall, centred.
That is the *only* full-width bottom band in the reference set.

**Measured from S3's flanking cards:** width 40% of frame (432px @1080), top edge at ~10%
of height (200px @1920), inset from its own edge by ~56px.

> ⚠️ The row weights above were previously ~45/35/15/5 with "large lower composite" second
> at 35%. That over-weighted the bottom band and, combined with it being the only mode
> given coordinates, produced builds where *every* graphic was a dark rounded rectangle
> across the bottom of frame. It is the rarest placement, not the second most common.

There is no equivalent of the old `DOCK-BL` 320px shelf anywhere in the references.

**Graphics are opaque.** Reference cards have a visible edge and a coloured bloom. A card
that is no longer the subject dims to ~30% but stays crisp. Nothing renders as a
translucent haze over the footage.

## 4. Captions — re-measured 2026-08

> ⚠️ **This section was wrong and has been corrected.** It previously recorded
> "~140–165px at 1080 wide" and that figure propagated into `ig-captions/SKILL.md`,
> `MOTION-LANGUAGE.md`, and `tokens-viral.css`. It appears to have measured the full
> height of a *two-line block including its glow* and recorded it as a glyph size.
> Captions shipped ~35% oversized and running edge to edge, which was the largest single
> cause of bad-looking output. Re-measured off full-resolution frames with
> `analysis/measure.py`; full working in `analysis/DEEP-ANALYSIS.md §5`.

| | Reference (measured) | What the wrong figure produced |
|---|---|---|
| Cap height @1080×1920 | **58–65px** body · **100px** hook | 93–100px everywhere |
| Font size | **88px** body · **140px** hook block | 140–165px everywhere |
| **Longest line width** | **50–62% of frame** | 72–87% |
| Words per line | **1–3** | 3–4 |
| Weight | Heavy condensed caps | correct |
| Legibility | **Glow** — coloured bloom matching the text colour, plus a soft dark halo | correct |
| Colour | White base, **fully saturated** accent | correct |
| Lines | 1–2, stacked tight, **each line can be a different colour** | correct |
| Band (block centre) | y **49–57%** of height | correct |

**The binding constraint is line width, not point size.** Every reference caption leaves
roughly 20% of the frame empty on each side. An 88px caption that still runs to the edges
looks as wrong as a 160px one.

> **House deviation:** this repo ships **single-line captions of 2–4 words**, with the
> width ceiling raised to 78% to accommodate them. The references use two-line blocks at
> 62%. That is a deliberate brand choice by the owner, not a misreading of the references.

**Accent frequency, measured: ~40% of blocks.** The other ~60% are plain white on both
lines. The accent goes on the *payload* words — it is the first line about as often as the
second.

Measured caption colours:

```
RED   (bad / old / myth / the thing to stop)   #EB0408
GREEN (good / new / fix / the thing to do)     #00E800
WHITE (everything else)                        #FFFFFF
```

Both are near-maximum saturation. The old palette's `#A33` and `#6A9E72` are muted by
comparison and read as timid next to these.

The colour is applied **per line as often as per word** — `STOP USING` entirely red above
`HOOKS` entirely white; `DISGUSTINGLY` entirely green above `EASY` entirely white.

## 5. Cut rhythm and graphic presence

| | S1 | S2 | S3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct visual events | 32 | 31 | 34 |
| Runtime | 45.5s | 47.6s | 51.0s |
| Mean interval | **1.4s** | **1.5s** | **1.5s** |
| Longest static beat | ~2.0s | ~2.0s | ~2.0s |
| **Runtime with NO graphic on screen** | **~0%** | **~10%** | **~15%** |

- A-roll shot changes every **1.5–3s**.
- A graphic state changes every **1–2s** — new layer, new field filled, new number.
- Whip-pan / motion-blur transitions on hard scene changes (S3 at 13s and 21s).
- **S1 never shows a bare talking head.** Its profile stage is present in every frame of
  the reel. (An earlier version of this file described S1 as alternating ~3s of graphic
  with ~3s of face — it does not; the graphic never leaves, the *content within it*
  changes.)
- Graphics **hold long enough to be read** — a pyramid layer sits ~2s. Nothing flashes.

> **The graphic layer is the default state of the frame. Bare footage is the exception.**
> Target ≥85% graphic presence. Most of the events above are state changes on an object
> already on screen, not new graphics — one persistent object mutating 20 times costs one
> composition.

## 6. The palette, measured

```
ground        #0A0A0B    near-black, slight blue
card          #16161A    UI surface (Instagram dark mode)
stroke        #2A2A31    1px UI borders
ink           #FFFFFF    pure white — these reels DO use pure white
ink-dim       #8E8E93    iOS secondary label grey
bad           #EB0408    vivid red
good          #00E800    vivid green
metric        #FFC531    gold — view counts, the winner, the highlight ring
link          #3B9DFF    iOS/IG link blue
```

Note two deliberate breaks from the old system: **pure white and pure black are fine here**,
and **the accent is not limited to one per frame** — a comparison frame legitimately shows
red and green and gold at once, because each is carrying a different meaning.

## 7. What to steal, in priority order

1. **Rebuild the app UI being discussed.** Highest value, most reusable, works for most
   business/creator content.
2. **Real content + metric badge, in pairs.** The comparison does the arguing.
3. **Semantic colour wash** on the footage. Nearly free, reads instantly.
4. **Charts with real axes and units.**
5. **Progressive build with persistent state** across multiple cutaways.
6. **Bigger, glowing, saturated captions.**
7. **CTA shown as an interaction** (a button pressed, a document opened).

## 8. What to stop doing

- Plates with a headline and a rule on them.
- Engraved serif micro-labels. Nothing in the references uses a serif anywhere.
- Restating the caption in a second typeface.
- Numbered empty slots standing in for content that was never named.
- Muted "tasteful" colour where the meaning is binary good/bad.
- Docking everything into the bottom 320px.
- Inventing an abstract instrument when the literal object is available.
